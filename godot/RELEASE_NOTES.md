## Shores O' Fortune for Android: ship parts

The sixteenth build opens the Shipwright's Refit tab: twenty parts in four
slots and five rarities, each one built onto her where you can see it, and
each one changing how she sails and fights.

- **Twenty parts.** Hull plating, sails, guns and the figurehead, from honest
  common shipwright's work to mythic pieces that are frankly not of this
  world. Oak rubbing strakes and iron straps climb to gilded dragonscale and
  the Leviathan Aegis, obsidian veined with light under hovering wards of
  glowing glass. Storm canvas and crimson-bound clipper cloth climb to sails
  embroidered with a golden phoenix and aether sails with storm light running
  through them. Bronze long guns climb to dragon-mouthed cannons and starfire
  lances, and an oak lion to a pearl-white seraph with a turning halo. The
  glowing ones come alive after dark.
- **The Refit tab.** Her figures as bars, her class's own in sea green and
  what her parts add in gold, then a track for each slot from the standard
  fit to the mythic part. Tap a step to read its card and see what it would
  change. If it's yours she's refitted with it while the camera comes round
  to that part of her. Parts you haven't looked at yet put a gold dot on the
  tab and a NEW badge on their step.
- **Where parts come from.** Every market now has a Shipwright's counter
  with three parts for sale, paid from your purse, and its wares change from
  day to day. Treasure chests and salvage sometimes hold one, and a lucky
  figurehead improves the odds. Now and then a pirate sails with a prize
  fitting of her own, salvaged from her wreck when she goes down. A part you
  already have is sold as a spare for gold, and a legendary or mythic find
  gets a toast all of its own.
- **Each ship her own refit.** The sloop, the brig and the galleon each keep
  their own parts. The harbour card shows her figures with them, the pips
  her parts add glowing gold, and how many she has fitted.
- **Built light.** Each part is laid into her own meshes, so a refit adds no
  draw calls (only the mythic pieces that float and turn have their own),
  and the finest work, like rivets and bolts, is only drawn up close.

Still aboard from the earlier builds: Puerto Marézia with its townsfolk, the
Shipwright with its flag brush and colour picker, the helmsman, the minimap
and the sea chart with your waypoints, the harbour where you choose your
ship, "Where shall we sail?", the pause screen and Settings, your progress
kept between plays, the trading ports with their markets and chandleries,
the open sea with its swells, sun glitter and turning day, the weather
(eight skies, storms with lightning, rain, mist and mirror calm), the night
with its stars, moon, lanterns and glowing seas, both archipelagos with
their islands, plants, shallows and surf, the pirates and their broadsides,
five kinds of shot and the shot pie, holes, broken masts, wrecks breaking in
two and wreckage afloat, plunder and your hold, the crews on deck, every
sound of the sea and the band's songs, and your farewell when the sea takes
you.
